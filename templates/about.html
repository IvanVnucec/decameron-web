about decameron

speed camera alerts usually come with a catch: an app that wants your location, an account, a subscription, a phone mounted on the windscreen

decameron does the same job with none of that. It's a small, independent hardware project: one device that knows where the cameras are, tells you about them, and keeps where you've been to itself.

{% endcall %} {% call m.section("light") %}

what we stand for

offline

No SIM, no Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth. Everything it needs is on the device, so it works the same in a city centre and on a mountain road with no signal.

private

No account, no app, no data collection. Your position is used for a moment to find the nearest camera, and then it's gone.

open

The firmware is MIT-licensed on GitHub, and the camera data comes from OpenStreetMap. Anyone can check it, improve it, or build their own.

pay once

Buy it once and it's yours. Updates are a public download, free forever, and don't depend on us staying in business.

who builds it

decameron is made by Ivan Vnučec, an embedded software engineer from Zagreb, Croatia. The idea came from a love of road trips across Europe.

The whole story, from the first prototype on, is on the project blog.
