how it works

the camera locations are stored on the device. GPS tells the device where it is on the road. when it gets close to a camera, it warns you with a light and a voice.

it doesn't need a phone, an app, or an internet connection — just a USB-C power source.

what happens on a drive

from the moment you plug it in to the moment you pass a camera.

  1. plug it in

    it starts looking for GPS. the first fix takes about 30 seconds — the GPS LED turns from red to green once the signal is found.

  2. while you drive

    a blinking green status LED means it's working.

  3. camera ahead

    the blue LED starts flashing and a voice announces the camera's speed limit.

  4. inside the camera radius

    it beeps. faster beeping means you're above the limit, slower means you're below it.

  5. after you pass the camera

    the warning stops and it's looking for the next camera.

installation takes a minute

first setup

connect the device to any USB-C power source and place it on or near the dashboard, speaker facing down. that's it — no app, no pairing, no configuration. if the sound is too loud or too quiet, turn the volume wheel on the side.

keeping it current

the camera list is open data from OpenStreetMap, with new cameras added daily. updating takes less than a minute: download the latest file, copy it to the device, and it restarts. no account, no subscription, no internet on the device.
