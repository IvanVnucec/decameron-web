how it works

the camera locations are stored on the device. GPS tells the device where it is on the road. when it gets close to a camera, it warns you with a light and a voice

{#- on-a-drive / lights-and-sounds: older links (support page, setup page, FAQ, the device section) land on the steps -#}

  1. plug it in

    A start-up sound, then “searching for a GPS signal”. The status LED blinks green to show decameron is running, and the GPS LED stays red until it finds satellites.

  2. GPS found

    Usually within about 30 seconds you hear “GPS signal acquired” and the GPS LED turns green: decameron is now watching the road.

  3. camera ahead

    About {{ alert_lookahead_s }} seconds before a camera: “warning, speed camera ahead, limit fifty”, and the camera LED flashes. Then it keeps beeping: three beeps if you're over the limit, one if you're under.

  4. camera passed

    As soon as you're moving away from the camera the beeps stop, and decameron waits for the next one.

good to know

what decameron does and doesn't do

fixed cameras only

It warns about fixed speed cameras mapped on OpenStreetMap. Mobile cameras, red-light cameras and average-speed sections are not in the list. See what's covered.

every camera nearby, in any direction

It warns about every camera it gets close to, including ones facing the other way or on a road next to yours. Better one warning too many than one too few.

needs a view of the sky

GPS works through a normal windscreen. Tunnels and garages block it; decameron tells you when the signal is lost and when it's back.

new cameras may be missing

New cameras can appear before anyone maps them. Keep an eye on the signs, and check the rules where you drive.

under the hood

hardware

  • Raspberry Pi RP2040 microcontroller
  • GPS receiver
  • 16 MB flash memory holding the firmware and the full camera list
  • speaker, with a volume wheel
  • status, GPS and camera LEDs
  • USB-C for power and updates

software & data

  • open-source firmware, MIT licensed, on GitHub
  • every night a build server downloads every speed camera from OpenStreetMap, together with its speed limit
  • the list is compiled into a new firmware file. you can browse the whole list on the coverage map
  • you update your decameron with one click in the browser, or by copying one file
